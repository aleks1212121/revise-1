Source clarifications:

- Oogonium chromatid number depends on cell-cycle stage. Primary and secondary oocyte chromosome counts are separated from chromatid counts; the diploid zygote is not labelled as a haploid meiotic product.
- Reserve counts, polar-body timing and cytoplasm percentages are approximate/context-dependent. Most follicles are lost without ovulation. Polar bodies have chromosomes and cannot supply a complete paternal/embryonic genetic test.
- The 28-day cycle and numbered feedback days are teaching models; actual cycle/ovulation timing varies. Early follicular growth is relatively gonadotrophin-independent, not unregulated.
- Dominance involves multiple factors. The AMH statement is a simplified stage-dependent influence, not a complete explanation. IVF follicle stimulation is distinguished from suppression of a premature LH surge.
- Trophoblastic hCG associated with implantation rescues the corpus luteum; fertilisation is not immediate rescue. Luteal decline does not impose one exact ten-day duration. Luteinisation raises progesterone without abolishing all oestrogen.
- The LH-surge slide wrongly suggests granulosa cells complete meiosis I: the primary oocyte does. The atresia activity concerns falling FSH support, not direct toxic effects of FSH.
- Basal temperature and LH kits provide indirect hormonal information, not proof of exact oocyte-release time or pregnancy. Cervical mucus comparisons are relative, not an absolute impervious barrier.
- Endometrial effects of hormonal contraception are not equated with termination of an implanted pregnancy or an established primary implantation-blocking mechanism. The apparent desogestrel “75 mg” unit error is not reproduced as a dosage fact. Product schedules, implant duration and dated effectiveness estimates are not universal current prescribing guidance.
- Menopause definitions exclude alternative causes/context; hormone use can obscure bleeding. Perimenopause is variable rather than uniformly low oestrogen, and not every cycle is anovulatory.
- HRT is not always combined. Systemic oestrogen generally requires endometrial protection when a uterus is present; regimen, route and individual factors change risks. No treatment selection or dosing protocol is provided.
- Cell-culture repair findings are not clinical skin-wound treatment evidence. Proposed postmenopausal immune roles and evolutionary accounts are labelled as research/hypotheses, not settled physiological or clinical claims.

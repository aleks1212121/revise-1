Source clarifications and gaps:

- Koch’s postulates are a historical causation framework with known limitations, not an absolute rule excluding carriage or organisms that cannot be grown independently. The cards teach the conceptual evidence, not experimental instructions.
- The lecture names immune evasion but supplies no specific capsule, complement or antibody-evasion mechanisms. These are not inferred from the heading.
- Numerical iron concentrations on page 9 have unclear formatting/units. Cards test binding/storage and availability rather than unreliable quantitative recall.
- Exotoxins are protein toxins; distant action is possible, not definitional. The species list described as producing a single toxin is simplified, not an exhaustive biological rule. Blank requests for plasmid examples are not guessed; β-phage/diphtheria is the supplied example.
- Diphtheria A/B function and EF-2 ADP-ribosylation follow the figures. Potency slogans such as one molecule killing one cell are not promoted into universal clinical thresholds. Iron-dependent repression concerns production, not treatment.
- Botulinum toxin has a disulphide bond, not a third disulphide chain. The source image supports presynaptic SNARE cleavage and impaired acetylcholine release. The foodborne case does not define all botulism. Classical toxin-type lists are not treated as a complete modern taxonomy.
- Tetanus slides identify CNS effects but do not show a detailed inhibitory-neuron pathway. No unsupported molecular pathway or treatment regimen is added. The lack of required extensive local injury is distinguished from claiming wounds never damage tissue.
- Cholera directly modifies Gαs, impairing its GTPase-dependent inactivation and sustaining adenylate cyclase/cAMP signalling. The old schematic’s Gi arrow must not be mistaken for the toxin target. The entry figure is simplified and is not expanded into an invented organelle-trafficking route. Clinical percentages/shedding figures are source-dependent examples, not fixed values for every case.
- Pages 39/42 incorrectly conflate Shiga-like toxin with cholera-like E. coli LT and overgeneralise Salmonella. The cards explicitly correct this: LT is heat-labile/cholera-like; Shiga-like toxins damage ribosomal RNA and inhibit protein synthesis. Staphylococcal enterotoxins are relatively heat stable; A–D is not an exhaustive modern list.
- Case histories illustrate mechanisms and clinical consequences, not stand-alone diagnostic rules or treatment advice. Original images have their source resolution; cropping does not restore absent detail.
